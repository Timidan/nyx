   Rules & Judging Policy
4. Native BOT Chain projects score higher
   BOT Chain welcomes existing Solidity projects, EVM DApps, and other on-chain apps to migrate and deploy here.
   In judging, projects built natively on BOT Chain — making deep use of BOT Chain's capabilities, or innovating around the BOT Chain ecosystem — receive additional weight.
   Projects that only complete a basic deployment, swap the RPC, do a simple migration, or make low-effort adaptations are eligible, but may score lower. The exact weighting is at the judging committee's discretion.
5. Similar or duplicate submissions
   Where multiple projects are highly similar in function, technical implementation, code structure, or overall approach, BOT Chain may evaluate them together as a group.
   In that case, judging weighs:
   Submission time
   Technical completeness
   Product completeness
   User experience
   Innovation
   Real contribution to the BOT Chain ecosystem
   For highly similar projects, BOT Chain may prioritize the one submitted earlier and of higher overall quality. BOT Chain has sole authority to determine what counts as "similar."
6. Prizes are maximum amounts — not guaranteed payouts
   The award counts and amounts shown are the maximum available for this event, not a guarantee. All prizes are awarded on quality, not by filling every listed slot.
   If a track, category, or award direction has no submissions that meet the standard, BOT Chain may:
   Reduce the number of winners;
   Adjust the amounts actually paid;
   Withhold certain awards;
   Retain or reallocate undistributed prize money to other categories.
7. Fair-play principles
   All participants must submit real, original, and verifiable work. The following may lead to disqualification:
   Plagiarism or unauthorized use of others' code, content, or projects;
   Fake demos, fake transactions, or fake data;
   Prize farming or mass duplicate submissions;
   Misleading materials intended to influence judging;
   Any other violation of fair competition.
   BOT Chain may request additional materials, including but not limited to: GitHub repo, demo, contract address, transaction records, and development documentation. Projects that can't be verified may be disqualified from judging.
8. Final interpretation rights
   BOT Chain retains final authority over this event, including but not limited to: rule interpretation, eligibility, submission validity, judging and results, prize distribution, and dispute resolution. Decisions of the BOT Chain judging committee are final and binding.
9. On AI development tools
   Using AI coding agents or AI development tools is allowed and encouraged — including but not limited to Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Cursor, and Windsurf.
   Participants must still ensure they:
   Hold the legal right to use what they submit;
   Can explain how the project is implemented;
   Can demo and verify the project.
   Judging still centers on project value, implementation quality, depth of BOT Chain integration, and real-world usability.

FAQ

1. Who can join?
   Solidity / EVM developers, builders with existing EVM projects, AI Agent developers, independent developers and startup teams, and technical users who want to try BOT Chain and give feedback. Solo or teams of 1–4.
2. Does it have to be a brand-new project?
   No. Deploying or connecting an existing Solidity project or EVM DApp to BOT Chain fully qualifies.
3. Does my project have to be open source?
   A GitHub repo is recommended so judges can understand the implementation. Whether to fully open-source is up to you.
4. Does it cost anything to join?
   No. Testnet deployment uses Faucet test tokens, which are free during the event.
5. Can I just give feedback without building a project?
   Yes — go through the PR / Bug / Optimization Bounty. The requirement is a reproducible issue + a clear cause + an actionable fix. Complaints without a solution aren't prioritized for payouts.
6. Can I submit something half-finished?
   Yes, but there must be a runnable demo the judges can verify. Pure concepts that don't run won't enter judging.
7. Can one person win multiple awards?
   Yes. The three categories — Track Awards, Best Content, and Bounty — are independent and can be won at the same time, with prizes stacked.
8. Do I have to post on X?
   Project submissions must post one X showcase tweet tagging @BOTChain_ai — it's required for judging — but one simple tweet is enough; marketing-grade content isn't required. The Best Content award is where quality matters.
9. Can I use AI to write code?
   Absolutely — we encourage it. See the prompt in Build Resources.
10. How, when, and in what are prizes paid?
    In USDT. Results are announced on Jul 15 along with wallet collection; after confirmation, finance pays out within two weeks.
11. Who are the judges?
    The BOT Chain internal team, with technical, product, or ecosystem members brought in for review where needed.
12. Who owns my project and code?
    You do. Joining the event does not transfer any intellectual property.
13. Where do I ask questions?
    Builders Telegram: https://t.me/BOTChainBuilders — technical Q&A is available during the event.
